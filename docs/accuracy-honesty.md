# Keeping the AI Honest (Plain English)

The chat answers questions two different ways, and the app tells you which one it used.

1. **"Computed from data"** — For questions like "which creators get the most engagement?", the
   AI doesn't guess. It asks a small piece of code to actually calculate the answer from the real
   spreadsheet of TikTok data, and then just puts that real number into a sentence. It's
   physically not able to make up a name or a number here — the answer has to match what's
   actually in the data.

2. **"AI opinion"** — For questions like "would this creator fit a skincare brand?", there's no
   spreadsheet calculation that can answer that — it's a judgment call. The AI gives you its best
   read, and the app labels it clearly as opinion, not fact, so you know to treat it as a
   starting point for your own judgment, not a verified number.

Why this matters: it would be easy for an AI tool to sound equally confident about both kinds of
answers, which is how people end up trusting a guess as if it were a fact. Labeling the two
differently — and doing it structurally, not just with a disclaimer sentence — means you always
know whether you're looking at a real number or a reasoned guess.
